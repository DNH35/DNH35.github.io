Resolution-invariant representation learning for time series

Signals arrive at different sampling rates, and most self-supervised time-series models quietly assume they do not. I developed a framework that pairs flow matching with STFT / Superlet time–frequency transforms, so the learned representation is tied to the signal's spectral structure rather than to its sampling grid. The objective supports multi-level supervision, letting a single pretrained backbone serve tasks at several granularities.

It outperforms masked-autoencoding and Fourier Neural Operator baselines across three different domains — neural decoding, sleep-stage classification, and human activity recognition — which is the part that mattered: the gain is not a single-dataset artifact. A grid search over feature-extraction window sizes and layer configurations added a further 20% on top.

  • Flow matching
  • STFT / Superlet
  • Neural operators
  • Multi-level supervision

NeurIPS 2025 Workshop · Spotlight Yue Lab & Dyer Lab

Positional encoding for neural time series without electrode geometry

Masked-autoencoding transformers on neural recordings typically need to know where each electrode sits in 3D space — a requirement that breaks transfer across subjects, rigs, and implant layouts. I designed a positional encoding that recovers the needed structure from the 1D time series alone, removing the dependence on electrode coordinates entirely.

Downstream classification and decoding improved 15% over supervised baselines, and the resulting model pretrains on datasets whose geometry was never recorded.

  • Self-supervised learning
  • Masked autoencoders
  • Transformers
  • Neural decoding

Signal research Independent

FGNO signal research for equity alpha

I pretrained an FGNO Transformer backbone with self-supervised flow matching on STFT spectrograms of daily equity price and volume data, then probed the frozen representations with 8 downstream regressors across a 36-way (layer, flow-time) grid to predict 5-day forward returns.

The mean-variance-optimized long/short signal came back at a pooled Sharpe of +2.07. I killed it. Overlapping 5-day labels induce autocorrelation that inflates naive significance; under Newey–West correction and a bootstrap the result did not survive, and walk-forward re-validation turned it into a Sharpe of −7 to −10. The finding here is the failure mode, and how cheaply a 36-way probe grid manufactures one that looks real.

  • Self-supervised pretraining
  • Newey–West
  • Bootstrap inference
  • Walk-forward validation
  • MVO
Generative modeling Independent

Trajectory flow matching for financial time series

I adapted Trajectory Flow Matching — a stochastic bridge model with a causal Transformer history encoder — to estimate the joint next-day distribution of equity returns, volatility, abnormal volume, and liquidity, sampled through Monte Carlo scenario generation. Joint, because those four quantities are what a position actually faces together.

Benchmarked against Neural SDE, probabilistic Transformer, and LightGBM quantile baselines over four expanding-window folds, scored on energy score, QLIKE, and rank IC. Backtests were dollar- and sector-neutral long/short, with turnover, transaction costs, market impact, and borrow fees charged against the P&L.

  • Stochastic bridges
  • Causal Transformers
  • Monte Carlo
  • Energy score / QLIKE
  • Neutralized backtests
